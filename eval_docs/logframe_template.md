# Logical framework template

A logframe is a four-row table that forces one question at each level: what
we will do, what that produces, what changes as a result, and what larger
change that contributes to. Its value is in the discipline of filling in the
columns, and in the assumptions column most of all, which is the one that
gets left blank.

`logframe_template.csv` is the same table as a file you can open in a
spreadsheet. `logframe_mhh_program.xlsx` is a completed example for a school
menstrual health programme.

## The table

| Level | Objective | Indicator | Baseline | Target | Means of verification | Assumptions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | The long-term change the programme contributes to but cannot deliver alone | An outcome measured at population level, usually from a source you do not control | From a published survey | Directional, not numeric, unless you are the main contributor | NFHS, ASER, census, administrative data | What else has to hold for outcomes to add up to this goal |
| **Outcome** | The change in behaviour, practice or condition among the people the programme reaches, that the programme is responsible for | Measured among participants and a comparison, at baseline and endline | From your baseline survey | Numeric, with the date by which | Your own survey, with the design that supports the comparison | What has to hold for outputs to produce this change: the programme's theory, stated as a condition |
| **Output** | What the programme delivers: trained, built, distributed, reached | Counts and coverage | Zero, usually | Numeric, per quarter or year | Monitoring records, attendance sheets, distribution logs | What has to hold for activities to produce outputs: staff in post, materials arrive, schools open |
| **Activity** | What staff do | Completion, on schedule | | Dates | Work plan, reports | Budget released, contracts signed |

## How to fill it in, in order

1. **Outcome first.** Not the goal, not the activities. The outcome is the
   change you are claiming responsibility for, and it decides everything
   else: what the activities have to be, and what the goal can honestly be.
2. **Goal second**, as the larger change your outcome is one contributor to.
   If you cannot name a contributor other than yourself, your goal is really
   an outcome and belongs a row down.
3. **Outputs third**, as the things that must exist for the outcome to be
   possible. Each output should be traceable to at least one outcome; an
   output nothing depends on is an activity somebody likes.
4. **Activities last.** They fall out of the outputs.
5. **Assumptions, at every level, before indicators.** The assumption between
   activities and outputs is operational (materials arrive). The assumption
   between outputs and outcomes is the programme's theory (trained peer
   leaders will run sessions, girls will attend, attending changes practice).
   The assumption between outcomes and goal is about the rest of the world.
   If the outputs-to-outcomes assumption cannot be stated in one sentence,
   the programme does not yet have a theory, and no indicator will supply it.
6. **Indicators last**, one or two per row. See `indicator_guidance.md` for
   what makes one usable and the errors that make one useless.

## Errors that recur

**Outputs dressed as outcomes.** "Number of girls trained" is an output.
"Share of girls using a sanitary product" is an outcome. A logframe whose
outcome row counts things the programme did is measuring effort and calling
it change.

**A goal indicator the programme cannot move.** "District stunting rate" is a
fine goal indicator and a poor performance measure: fifty programmes and the
monsoon move it. Do not put a target on it that anyone will be held to.

**Baseline blank.** A target with no baseline is a wish. If the baseline will
come from a survey you have not run yet, write "baseline survey, month X" in
the cell and a date, not nothing.

**Assumptions written as risks.** "Risk: schools may not cooperate" is a risk.
The assumption is "schools will cooperate", and it belongs next to the
output it is a condition for, so that when a school does not cooperate the
logframe shows which output is now in question.

**Means of verification that do not exist.** "Endline survey" is a means of
verification only if there is a budget line and a date for it. Check the
column against the budget.
