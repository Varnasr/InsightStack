# Miro

A human-centred design session outline and links to boards worth copying from.
No board file ships: a Miro board lives in Miro and is copied from a template
there, so what a repository can usefully hold is the session design.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `miro_use_case_india.md` | When a board is worth the setup, and when a room and paper is faster |
| `HCD_Miro.pdf` | A session structure: framing, empathy mapping, ideation, prioritisation |
| `Miro HCD Template Example.png` | What the finished board looks like |
| `Miro Purpose.png` | The framing slide |
| `sample_miro_board_links.txt` | Public boards to copy from |

## When to use it

A remote or hybrid workshop where participants are in three cities and the
alternative is a shared slide deck. Miro's sticky-note-and-vote workflow is a
good substitute for a wall and a stack of Post-its when there is no wall.

## When not to

A workshop where everyone is in the room. Paper is faster, needs no login, and
the people least comfortable with a laptop are often the people whose input
matters most. In a physical room Miro makes the facilitator the only person who can
write on the wall.

And anything that needs to survive: a Miro board is a snapshot on a
third-party server, and the free plan limits how many you can keep. Export the
board to PDF at the end of the session and put the PDF somewhere you control.

## What goes wrong

Participants on a phone. Miro on a small screen is unusable for anything beyond
reading, so a session where some people are joining from the field needs a
second channel for them, or their contribution disappears.
