# annotated_research

Five research briefs marked up with one annotation scheme. Eight tags, each
a question to put to a research note: what is it asking, what is the
comparison, who is in the data, what was measured, is the arithmetic right
for the design, does it name its weaknesses, does the conclusion's verb match
the design, who consented. `annotation_scheme.md` defines them.

| Brief | Design | Main lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `briefs/01_school_health_clubs.md` | Before-after, one arm | A year of growing up is inside the estimate |
| `briefs/02_remedial_reading.md` | Difference-in-differences, matched | The baseline table contradicts the identifying assumption |
| `briefs/03_flood_early_warning.md` | Post-event cross-section | The households not interviewed are the outcome |
| `briefs/04_shg_savings.md` | Randomised by village | Differential attrition and unclustered errors on a good design |
| `briefs/05_cash_transfer_nutrition.md` | Regression discontinuity | A credible local estimate extended past the population it identifies |

Ordered from weakest design to strongest.

All five are illustrative. The programmes, districts, organisations and
numbers are invented. No brief describes or cites a real study. The original
`annotated_research_brief_mhh.pdf` is kept as it was.

To teach with them, hand out a brief without its annotations, have readers
tag it with the scheme, and compare. Where two readers tag the same sentence
differently, the text is ambiguous.
