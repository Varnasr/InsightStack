# annotated_research

Five research briefs marked up to show where they are strong and where they
are wrong, with one annotation scheme applied to all of them.

The point is the scheme. Eight tags, each a question to put to any research
note that lands on your desk: what is it really asking, what is the
comparison, who is in the data, what was measured, is the arithmetic right for
the design, does it name its real weaknesses, does the conclusion's verb match
the design, and who consented. `annotation_scheme.md` defines them and says
how to teach with them.

| Brief | Design | Main lesson |
|---|---|---|
| `briefs/01_school_health_clubs.md` | Before-after, one arm | A year of growing up is inside the estimate |
| `briefs/02_remedial_reading.md` | Difference-in-differences, matched | The baseline table contradicts the identifying assumption |
| `briefs/03_flood_early_warning.md` | Post-event cross-section | The households not interviewed are the outcome |
| `briefs/04_shg_savings.md` | Randomised by village | Differential attrition and unclustered errors on a good design |
| `briefs/05_cash_transfer_nutrition.md` | Regression discontinuity | A credible local estimate, extended past the population it identifies |

Ordered from weakest design to strongest, so a reader who works through them
in sequence sees the ceiling on what each design can claim rise, and the
errors change from fatal to specific.

**All five are illustrative.** The programmes, districts, organisations and
numbers are invented to make the teaching points. No brief describes a real
study or cites one. They are written the way such notes are actually written,
which is why each contains the errors real notes contain.

The original `annotated_research_brief_mhh.pdf` is the example this folder
started with and is kept as it was.

## Using them

Hand out a brief without its annotations. Have readers tag it themselves
using the scheme, then compare. Where two readers tag the same sentence
differently, the text is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is usually where the
misleading claim lives.
