# Brief 3: Community flood early warning in a riverine district

*Illustrative. The programme, the district and every number are invented for
teaching. Annotations in blockquotes use the tags in `../annotation_scheme.md`.*

---

## Summary

A community-based flood early warning system was established in 60 villages
along a major river in a north-eastern state in 2023, with trained village
volunteers, a river-gauge SMS alert chain and pre-agreed evacuation points.
During the August 2024 flood, a household survey in the programme villages
found that 87 per cent of households received a warning at least six hours
before water entered the village, 91 per cent evacuated livestock, and no
lives were lost. The system is a proven model for flood-prone districts.

> **[FRAME]** The question a reader wants answered is whether the system
> reduced harm compared with what would have happened without it. The brief
> answers a different question: what happened in the villages that had it.
> Those can look identical on the page and they are not the same question.

> **[CLAIM]** "Proven model." Hold that phrase against the design below.

## Design and data

A rapid household survey was conducted in the 60 programme villages three
weeks after the flood peak. Enumerators visited each village and interviewed
the heads of households present at the time of the visit, targeting 15
households per village (n = 842 completed interviews). The survey asked when
the household received a warning, from whom, what actions it took, and what
losses it suffered.

> **[DESIGN]** Programme villages only. There is no non-programme village in
> the data, so nothing in the results can be compared to anything. "87 per
> cent received a warning six hours before" is meaningful only next to the
> figure for villages without the system, and that figure was not collected.
> The state's own gauge-based SMS alerts go to every village with a
> functioning mobile network; some of the 87 per cent is the state system,
> not the programme.

> **[SAMPLE]** "Households present at the time of the visit", three weeks after
> a major flood. The households not present are the ones still displaced, and
> the ones still displaced are disproportionately the ones who did not
> evacuate in time, lost their house, or lost the livestock they would have
> had to leave. The survey interviewed the households the flood treated
> best. This is survivorship in its most literal form, and every outcome in
> the table is affected by it in the same direction.

> **[SAMPLE]** 842 of a targeted 900 is 94 per cent completion, which sounds
> good and is the wrong denominator. The right denominator is the number of
> households in those 60 villages before the flood, and the brief does not
> report it.

> **[MEASURE]** "No lives were lost" is a claim about the population, sourced
> from a sample of the households that stayed. Deaths are counted by the
> district administration, and the brief should cite that count rather than
> infer zero from its own survey.

## Results

| Indicator | Programme villages (n=842) |
|---|---|
| Received a warning at least 6 hours before inundation | 87% |
| Warning received from the village volunteer | 64% |
| Warning received from state SMS alert | 58% |
| Evacuated livestock before inundation | 91% |
| Moved to the pre-agreed evacuation point | 73% |
| Reported loss of stored grain | 22% |
| Reported death or injury in the household | 0% |

> **[MEASURE]** "Received a warning from the village volunteer" and "from state
> SMS" sum to 122 per cent, so most households received both. The programme's
> distinctive contribution is the 29 per cent (87 minus 58) who got a warning
> only through the volunteer chain. That number is in the table and the brief
> does not compute it.

> **[ANALYSIS]** Percentages with no uncertainty, from a sample clustered in 60
> villages, of a population of unknown size. A confidence interval is not
> optional here and it would be wide, but the deeper problem is that no
> interval fixes a sample that excludes the worst-affected.

## Limitations

The survey was conducted rapidly under post-disaster conditions and could not
reach every household. Recall of the exact timing of warnings may be
imprecise.

> **[LIMIT]** "Could not reach every household" is true and understated: the
> households not reached are the study's missing outcome. "Recall may be
> imprecise" is the least important thing wrong with a recall of whether
> your house flooded three weeks ago.

## Conclusion

The community early warning system delivered timely warnings to nearly nine
in ten households and enabled protective action, with no loss of life. The
model is recommended for replication across the state's flood-prone blocks.

> **[CLAIM]** The design supports: among households that stayed in programme
> villages, most reported receiving a warning, most from more than one source,
> and most reported protective action. It does not support "delivered",
> "enabled", "no loss of life" or "recommended for replication". A comparison
> with non-programme villages on the same river, from the district's own
> loss records rather than a post-flood survey, would support a claim, and
> could be done from existing data at almost no cost.

> **[ETHICS]** Interviewing flood-affected households three weeks after the
> event, about their losses, by an organisation that is simultaneously
> distributing relief in those villages, raises the question of whether a
> household could decline. The brief does not address it. The 94 per cent
> completion rate is consistent with households who did not feel they could.

---

*What the annotated reader takes away: a survey of who is left after a
disaster, in the places that had the programme, measures the programme's best
case by construction. The comparison that would answer the question is in the
district administration's records and was not requested.*
