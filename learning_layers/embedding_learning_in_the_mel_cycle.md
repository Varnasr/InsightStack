# Embedding learning in the MEL cycle

Most MEL systems collect a great deal and change nothing. The data arrive,
the report is written, the report is filed. "Learning" appears in the acronym
and in a section heading and nowhere in the calendar.

This note is about the calendar. A learning system is a set of decisions,
each with a date, a person, and the evidence that will be in front of them.
If any of the three is missing, the decision will be made the way it would
have been made without the data.

## The unit is a decision, not a finding

Start from the decisions the programme will actually take in the next year.
Not the ones it could take: the ones that are already scheduled or that
recur. Typically:

- Which schools or villages get the next round of the intervention
- Whether to keep, change or drop a component
- How field staff time is allocated across sites
- What goes in the next proposal
- Whether to continue at all

For each, write down who takes it, when, and what evidence would change it.
The last part is the test: if no result could change the decision, it is
not a decision the MEL system should be serving, and collecting data for it
is documentation.

## Match the evidence to the decision's tempo

| Decision | Tempo | Evidence that can reach it in time | Evidence that cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reallocate field staff across sites | Monthly | Monitoring data: sessions held, attendance, the field checks in FieldStack `field_ops/` | An endline survey |
| Change a component | Quarterly | Monitoring trends; a rapid qualitative check on why a component is or is not working | An impact estimate |
| Continue or scale | Annually or at the end | The evaluation | Anything not designed with a comparison group |
| What goes in the next proposal | Once | The evaluation plus everything above | |

The commonest failure is an evaluation designed to answer the annual
question and then expected to inform the monthly ones. It cannot; the data
arrive a year late for that. The second commonest is monitoring data used to
answer the annual question, which it cannot either; it has no comparison.

## The three layers

**Layer 1, operational.** Monitoring data, reviewed monthly by the people who
can act on it, in a form they can act on. A one-page sheet: what was
delivered against plan, by site, with the outliers marked. The review is a
meeting with a fixed agenda, and its output is a list of changes to the next
month's plan. If the meeting produces no changes three months running, either
the programme is running perfectly or the sheet is not showing anything
anyone can act on. Assume the second.

**Layer 2, adaptive.** Quarterly. Whether the components are working as the
theory of change said they would, using the results chain
(`../writing_guides/results_chain.md`): which links are holding and which
are not. Evidence is monitoring trends plus deliberate short qualitative
work on the weak link. The output is a decision to keep, change or drop a
component, and a record of why, so that the endline can be read against
what was actually delivered rather than what was planned.

**Layer 3, evaluative.** Once, or at a milestone. Did it work. Evidence is
the evaluation, with the design that supports the claim. The output feeds
the continue-or-scale decision and the next design. This layer is the one
everyone plans for and the only one most programmes have, and on its own it
arrives too late to change anything about the programme it evaluates.

## What each layer needs from the other two

Layer 3 needs Layer 2's record of what changed and when, or it evaluates a
programme that no longer exists. Layer 2 needs Layer 1's data to see trends.
Layer 1 needs Layer 3's baseline to know what a normal month looks like. A
MEL framework that plans Layer 3 alone has planned a document.

## The learning agenda, as a page

One page, revised quarterly:

1. The five decisions and their dates
2. For each, the evidence that will be in front of the person taking it,
   and who is responsible for it being there
3. The weakest link in the results chain, and what is being done this
   quarter to find out whether it holds
4. The three things learned last quarter and what changed because of them

If item 4 is empty two quarters running, the system is not learning, and the
honest response is to stop collecting whatever feeds nothing.

## What this is not

It is not a knowledge management system, which is about storing and finding
documents (`../KM_tools/`). It is not a theory of learning organisations. It
is a calendar with names on it, and it works to the extent that the meetings
happen and produce changes.
