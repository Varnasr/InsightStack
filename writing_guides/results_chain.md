# Writing a results chain

A results chain is the theory of change with the arrows made explicit: each
link is a sentence of the form "if A, then B, because C". Written out, it
exposes the step that everyone assumed and nobody stated, which is where the
programme usually fails.

## The form

Inputs, then activities, then outputs, then outcomes, then impact. Between
each pair, a link written as a conditional with its mechanism:

> If peer leaders are trained and given materials (output), then monthly
> sessions will be held in each school (output), because trained peer leaders
> have the content and the school has agreed to the timetable.

> If monthly sessions are held (output), then girls in classes 6 to 8 will
> know the recommended hygiene practices (outcome), because the sessions
> cover them and girls attend.

> If girls know the practices (outcome), then more girls will use a sanitary
> product during menstruation (outcome), because knowing what to do is what
> stops them.

Read the third link again. It claims knowledge is the binding constraint. If
the real constraint is that pads cost money or that there is nowhere to
change one at school, the chain breaks at that link and no amount of
training fixes it. That is the sentence the exercise exists to produce.

## Rules

**One "because" per link, and it names a mechanism.** "Because the programme
is well designed" is not a mechanism. "Because knowing what to do is what
stops them" is a mechanism, and it can be wrong, which is the point.

**Every link is a hypothesis somebody could test.** If a link cannot be false,
it is not carrying any weight. "If sessions are held, then sessions will have
been held" is a tautology; "if sessions are held, then girls will attend" can
fail and the programme has to say why it will not.

**The weakest link decides the programme.** Find it, say it is the weakest,
and put the monitoring there. A results chain with no weak link has been
written to reassure rather than to plan.

**Outcomes are changes among people, not things the programme did.** "Girls
trained" is an output whatever column it is written in.

**Stop at the level the programme can be held to.** Impact, the population-
level change many programmes contribute to, belongs at the top as context.
The programme's claim ends at the outcome.

## From chain to logframe

The outputs and outcomes become the rows of the logframe
(`../eval_docs/logframe_template.md`). The "because" clauses become the
assumptions column. Nothing in the logframe should be absent from the chain,
and the chain should have nothing the logframe leaves out except the
mechanisms, which the logframe compresses into assumptions.

## A test

Give the chain to someone who has not seen the programme and ask them to
find the link they believe least. If they pick a different one from you,
one of you is wrong about the programme, and it is cheaper to find out now.
