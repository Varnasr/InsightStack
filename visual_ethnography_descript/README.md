# Working with recorded fieldwork

A transcript and a storyboard showing how a recording becomes material for
analysis. Descript is named because it transcribes and lets you cut the
recording by editing the text; the method applies to any transcription tool.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `climate_field_transcript.txt` | An interview about a flood season with a farming household. Illustrative |
| `descript_storyboard.md` | The sequence from recording to clips to quotes, and what is decided at each step |

## Method

Consent for the recording is separate from consent for the interview, and
consent to show a clip at a workshop is separate again. Ask for all three;
the third can be withheld. The storyboard has the wording.

Transcribe everything; quote sparingly. Automatic transcription of Hindi or
Bangla in a noisy courtyard is about 80 per cent accurate, which finds the
passages and does not quote them. Check every quoted passage against the
audio with someone who speaks the language.

Cut for the respondent's meaning. A twelve-second clip in which a farmer says
the embankment failed misrepresents her if the next forty seconds explain
that her own village removed the sluice gate. A clip is approved with its
context visible.

A voice, a face and a description of the field next to the embankment all
identify the household. Anonymising a recording is a decision about whether
the material can be shown, and to whom.

## Use

Learning products, workshop material, a field visit that could not happen.
One household's account is an illustration of a finding established
elsewhere, and the report should say so.
