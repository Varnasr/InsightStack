# Working with recorded fieldwork

A transcript, and a storyboard that shows how a recording becomes analysable
material. Descript is the tool named because it transcribes and lets you cut
the recording by editing the text, but the method is the same in any
transcription tool, and most of it is about what happens before the
recording is made.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `climate_field_transcript.txt` | A transcript of an interview about a flood season with a farming household. Illustrative |
| `descript_storyboard.md` | The sequence from recording to clips to quotes, and what is decided at each step |

## The method

**Consent covers the recording, and it is a different consent.** Agreeing to
be interviewed is not agreeing to be recorded, and agreeing to be recorded is
not agreeing to have a clip of your voice shown at a workshop. Three
consents, asked separately, and the third can be withheld while the first
two are given. The storyboard has the wording.

**Transcribe everything, quote sparingly.** Automatic transcription of Hindi
or Bangla speech in a noisy courtyard is around 80 per cent accurate on a
good day, which is good enough to find the passages and not good enough to
quote. Every quoted passage is checked against the audio by someone who
speaks the language. Descript makes this fast because the transcript is
time-linked; it does not make it optional.

**Cut for the respondent's meaning, not the report's.** A twelve-second clip
in which a farmer says the embankment failed is compelling and may be
exactly what she said. If the following forty seconds explain that it
failed because her own village removed the sluice gate, the clip is a
misrepresentation and the fact that every word in it is hers does not
change that. The storyboard's rule is that a clip is cut with its context
visible to whoever approves it.

**Recorded material identifies people.** A voice is identifying; a face is
identifying; a description of the field next to the embankment identifies
the household to everyone in the village. Anonymisation of a recording is
not bleeping a name. It is a decision about whether the material can be
shown at all, and to whom.

## What this is for

Learning products, workshop material, a donor's field visit that could not
happen. It is not evidence in the sense an evaluation needs: one household's
account, chosen because it was vivid, is an illustration of a finding
established elsewhere, and the report should say so.
